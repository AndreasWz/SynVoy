Find a gene in other species by looking at its neighbours

Some genes change so fast that a sequence search no longer finds them in related species. The genes next to them usually change much less. SynVoy finds that neighbourhood first and then looks for your gene inside it.

No account needed. You can also paste a sequence on the next page.

This copy of the site shows finished searches only. To run your own, install SynVoy from the source code.

    Three steps

    1

    Name a gene

    Enter a protein accession or paste a protein sequence. SynVoy looks up the species it comes from.

    2

    Pick the species to search

    Choose from the genomes on this server, closest relatives first. A species that is not there can be named: its genome is fetched from NCBI.

    3

    Read the answer

    One line per species: found, candidate, or not found, and how much of the neighbourhood is there. Figures and files are there for whoever wants the detail.

    Finished searches

    Real searches, shown as they came out. The outcome depends on the gene: long proteins in related species are found almost everywhere, short peptides rarely are. The bar shows the share of genomes with the gene found, with a candidate, and with neither.

    What this server does

    It searches the genomes kept here, and fetches others from NCBI when you name them. A limited number per search. A search takes minutes to hours, so you get a link to come back to.

    What it does not do

    It takes no uploaded genomes and no large batches. For those, and for data that must stay private, install SynVoy on your own machine or cluster. The same code runs there.
